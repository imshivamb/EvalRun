### **3. Detailed Day-by-Day Itinerary**

#### **PART 1: SOUTH KOREA (Days 1–7)**
*Base: Seoul (Hongdae / Yeonnam-dong)*

* **Day 1: Arrival & Yeonnam-dong Coffee Scene**
  * *Logistics:* Incheon Airport AREX All-Stop Train to Hongik Univ. Station (~₩4,500 / 55 mins).
  * *Afternoon/Work:* Check-in, grab coffee at Gyeongui Line Forest Park (Yeontral Park).
  * *Evening Photography:* Hongdae busking streets and neon-lit alleys.
* **Day 2: Seongsu-dong (The Brooklyn of Seoul) & Seoul Forest**
  * *Focus:* Industrial brick warehouses converted into design studios, specialty roasters (Center Coffee, Daelim Changgo), and pop-up thrift boutiques.
  * *Thrift Highlight:* Seongsu vintage curations along Yeonmujang-gil.
* **Day 3: Deep Heritage vs. Raw Vintage: Dongmyo & Euljiro**
  * *Morning:* Dongmyo Flea Market (*Closed/Quiet on wet days, bustling daily 10:00–18:00*). Unfiltered mountain of vintage clothing and analog cameras.
  * *Afternoon:* Euljiro ("Hip-jiro") print-shop alleys, retro hidden 4th-floor cafés (e.g., Coffee Hanyakbang).
  * *Evening:* Cheonggyecheon Stream walk for night photography.
* **Day 4: Mullae-dong Arts Village & Yeouido River Walk**
  * *Focus:* Active steel-working district interspersed with artist studios, micro-bakeries, and metalwork murals.
  * *Evening:* Sunset at Han River Park with instant ramen from convenience store dispensers.
* **Day 5: Bukchon / Ikseon-dong Alleyways & Remote Work Day**
  * *Morning (Remote Work):* Work from a traditional Hanok café with high-speed Wi-Fi in Ikseon-dong.
  * *Afternoon:* West village (Seochon) indie bookshops and street-level photo walks away from palace tourist crowds.
* **Day 6: Day Trip to Suwon Hwaseong Fortress**
  * *Logistics:* Seoul Metro Line 1 to Suwon (~₩2,000 / 45 mins).
  * *Focus:* Walk the 5.7 km historic fortress walls; explore Haenggung-dong café street (low-rise residential architecture, specialty drip coffee).
* **Day 7: Itaewon Antique Street, Hannam-dong & Night Flight Prep**
  * *Morning:* Itaewon Antique Furniture Street & Hannam-dong designer concept stores.
  * *Evening:* Pack bags, sunset at Naksan Park for Seoul city wall panoramic view.

#### **PART 2: JAPAN — EAST TO WEST (Days 8–29)**

##### **BASE 2: TOKYO (Days 8–15)**
*Base: East Tokyo (Kuramae / Asakusa) or West Tokyo (Koenji)*

* **Day 8: Flight Seoul (ICN/GMP) → Tokyo (NRT/HND) & Kuramae Check-in**
  * *Transit:* Keisei Access Express to Asakusa/Kuramae (~¥1,300).
  * *Vibe:* Kuramae is Tokyo’s artisan and craft-coffee quarter along the Sumida River.
* **Day 9: Shimokitazawa & Sangenjaya (Thrift & Indie Vinyl)**
  * *Focus:* The thrift capital of Tokyo. Browse Flamingo, Chicago, Toyo Department Store.
  * *Photography:* Narrow labyrinthine roads, indie live-houses, and hidden second-floor kissaten (retro coffee shops).
* **Day 10: Koenji & Nakano Broadway**
  * *Focus:* 1970s punk heritage, thrift shops along PAL Arcade, retro gaming and subculture photography at Nakano Broadway.
  * *Evening Work Spot:* Work-friendly cafés near Koenji Central Park.
* **Day 11: Yanaka, Nezu & Sendagi (Old Shitamachi Tokyo)**
  * *Focus:* Tokyo’s historic pre-war residential streets, Yanaka Ginza street snacks (croquettes, roasted sweet potatoes), and cemetery cat photography.
* **Day 12: Day Trip to Kamakura & Enoshima Coast**
  * *Logistics:* JR Shonan-Shinjuku Line (~¥950 / 1 hr). Walk the Enoden coastal train line for iconic retro seaside shots.
* **Day 13: Daikanyama & Meguro River Architectural Walk**
  * *Focus:* Daikanyama T-Site (award-winning Tsutaya Bookstore & design library, work-friendly), Nakameguro canal-side cafés, and vintage clothing stalls.
* **Day 14: Jimbocho Book Town & Kanda Kissaten**
  * *Focus:* Over 150 vintage and antiquarian bookstores, wood-paneled coffee houses (Mil-lisa, Glitch Coffee).
* **Day 15: Omoide Yokocho & Night Transit to Kansai**
  * *Evening:* Golden Gai & Omoide Yokocho street photography.
  * *Logistics Hack:* Take the **Willer Express Overnight Bus** to Osaka (Departs ~23:00, arrives ~06:30; saves ¥7,000 on a hotel night and beats bullet train fares).

  * *Focus:* By-pass tourist masses; head to Uji for matcha plantations and riverside work/café spots; explore Fushimi's canal-side wooden sake breweries.

  * *Focus:* Kamo River delta, Kyoto University student quarters, Hyakumanben Chion-ji area, and retro artisan bakeries.

  * *Focus:* Naramachi old merchant quarter (skip only feeding deer; explore preserved machiya houses and indie craft stores).

* **Day 23: Day Trip to Kurashiki Bikan Historical Quarter**
  * *Logistics:* Local JR train or regional bus to Okayama/Kurashiki.

### **4. Practical Logistics & Remote Work Tips**

1. **Connectivity:**
   * South Korea has the world's most accessible open Wi-Fi, but buy an **eSIM (e.g., LG U+ or Airalo, ~₹1,500 for 10GB/unlimited)** for uninterrupted hotspotting.
   * In Japan, use **Ubigi eSIM** (operates on NTT Docomo network, reliable on remote trains).
2. **Work-Friendly Cafés:**
   * *Korea:* Cafés welcome laptops (look for *Ediya Coffee*, *A Twosome Place*, or local indie roasteries with multi-plug tables).
   * *Japan:* Independent *kissaten* often disallow laptops; use chains like **Doutor, Komeda's Coffee**, or community co-working spaces inside hostels (e.g., UNPLAN Kagurazaka or Nui. Hostel in Tokyo).
3. **Thrifting Customs:**
   * Korea (Dongmyo): Cash is king. Bargaining is acceptable only on mixed clothing piles.
   * Japan (Shimokitazawa/Koenji): Fixed pricing. Most items are graded, dry-cleaned, and curated. Carry a passport for tax-free exemptions on purchases over ¥5,500.
